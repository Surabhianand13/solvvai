SIP formula

FV = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i) P = monthly SIP · i = yearly return ÷ 12 ÷ 100 · n = months

Example: ₹10,000 a month for 20 years at 12% grows to about ₹99.9 lakh. You invest ₹24 lakh; the rest is compounding.

How to use this SIP calculator

  1. Enter the amount you will invest every month.
  2. Enter the expected yearly return. Use 10–12% for equity funds and 6–8% for debt funds.
  3. Set how many years you will stay invested.
  4. Optionally add inflation to see what the final amount is worth in today’s money.

Why starting early matters so much

Two people each invest ₹10,000 a month at 12% until age 60:

  • Starts at 25 (35 years): about ₹6.50 crore, from ₹42 lakh invested.
  • Starts at 35 (25 years): about ₹1.90 crore, from ₹30 lakh invested.

Ten extra years means only ₹12 lakh more invested, but more than three times the final value. That’s compounding.

How to start a SIP

  1. Complete your KYC once with PAN and Aadhaar (online, through any fund house or app).
  2. Choose funds that match your goal and time horizon. Index funds are a simple low-cost start.
  3. Pick a SIP date just after your salary credit and set up an auto-debit mandate.
  4. Increase the SIP every year with your income. See the step-up SIP calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. Mutual fund returns depend on the market. The calculator shows what happens at a steady rate; real returns go up and down.

How are SIP gains taxed?

Equity fund gains held over 12 months are long-term and taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year. Short-term gains are taxed at 20%.

Can I stop or pause a SIP?

Yes, anytime, without penalty. Units already bought stay invested.

What is the minimum amount for a SIP?

Many funds allow SIPs from ₹100 or ₹500 a month.

Is SIP better than a lumpsum?

A SIP spreads your purchases over time, which smooths out market ups and downs. If you already have a large amount, a lumpsum can earn more over long periods. Compare with the lumpsum calculator.

Can I lose money in a SIP?

Yes, in the short term, because equity markets fall at times. Over 7+ years, diversified equity funds have rarely given negative returns, but past performance does not guarantee future returns.
