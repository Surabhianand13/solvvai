FD maturity formula

Maturity = P × (1 + r ÷ (100 × n))^(n × t) P = deposit · r = yearly rate · n = compounding per year (4 = quarterly) · t = years

Example: ₹1 lakh for 5 years at 7%, compounded quarterly, grows to ₹1,41,478, which is ₹41,478 interest and an effective yield of 7.19% a year.

TDS on FD interest and Form 15G/15H

  • Banks deduct 10% TDS when your FD interest at that bank crosses ₹50,000 in a year (₹1 lakh for senior citizens). Without a PAN, TDS is 20%.
  • If your total income is below the taxable limit, submit Form 15G (below 60) or Form 15H (60 and above) at the start of the year to avoid TDS.
  • TDS is not the final tax. FD interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate when you file your return.

FD laddering: a simple strategy

Instead of one big FD, split your money across 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5-year FDs. Each year one matures, giving you liquidity, and you can reinvest it at the 5-year rate. You lock in long-term rates without tying up everything.

Frequently asked questions

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes, at your income tax slab rate. Banks deduct 10% TDS once yearly interest crosses ₹50,000 (₹1 lakh for senior citizens) unless you submit Form 15G/15H.

What is a tax-saving FD?

A 5-year lock-in FD whose deposit (up to ₹1.5 lakh) can be claimed under Section 80C in the old tax regime. Interest is still taxable.

Are FDs safe?

Deposits up to ₹5 lakh per bank (principal + interest) are insured by DICGC. Small finance banks pay more but check their ratings.

Cumulative or payout FD?

Cumulative FDs reinvest interest and earn more. Payout FDs give regular income, which suits retirees.

Which is better: FD or RD?

An FD invests a lump sum at once; an RD builds savings monthly. With the same rate, an FD earns more because the full amount earns interest from day one. See our RD calculator.

Can I break an FD early?

Yes. Most banks allow premature withdrawal with a penalty of 0.5–1% on the rate applicable for the period the FD actually ran.

How is FD maturity calculated?

Maturity = P × (1 + r/400)^(4 × years) for quarterly compounding. The calculator above handles every compounding option.
